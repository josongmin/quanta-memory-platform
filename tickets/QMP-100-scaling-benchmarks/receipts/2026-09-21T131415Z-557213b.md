## RED evidence

- The first post-hardening smoke reused a stale optimized core artifact and
  reported 64,000 allocated bytes on each burst admission. A forced rebuild
  proved that result was not the current scheduler implementation and therefore
  invalidated the earlier performance receipt as proof of the cached hot path.
- The scaling lane checked final allocator drain but did not independently
  require the admission loop itself to remain allocation-free. A future
  reintroduction of Hamilton table construction in `admits` could therefore
  pass the zero-residual check.
